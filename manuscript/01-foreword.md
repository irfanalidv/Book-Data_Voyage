# A Note Before You Begin

*Data Voyage: Building Real AI Systems from Data to Deployment*


I do not have an endorsement from a famous professor or a Silicon Valley executive. I never tried to get one. Those front-matter blurbs are usually written by people who skimmed the book and agreed as a favour. They do not tell you anything real.

What I can tell you is why I wrote this.

I have spent the last several years building AI systems: at companies, for clients, under contract, and on my own. Some of it went well. Some of it went badly in ways that were expensive and instructive. Through all of it, I kept running into the same problem: the books and courses that exist teach you concepts in isolation, on toy datasets, with none of the friction that makes real work hard.

I wanted a book that taught you to build things that work. Not a book that teaches you what gradient descent is. A book that teaches you how to know when you need gradient descent, how to implement it correctly on data you collected yourself, how to deploy the result somewhere real, and how to charge for it.

That book did not exist when I needed it. So I wrote it.

The project running through every chapter, TalentLens, a job market intelligence platform, is real in the sense that matters: the data comes from real sources, the problems it solves are problems real people have, and the code is the kind of code you would write at work. It hasn't been cleaned up for a textbook or simplified until it stops resembling the problem.

I wrote this from Siliguri, a city in West Bengal near the Nepal border. Not from Bangalore. Not from a startup hub. From a room, with a laptop, with the same internet connection and the same package documentation as everyone else. If you are reading this from somewhere people tell you is too far from the action, it is not. The work is the thing that matters. This book is about the work.

Build something real. That is the whole point.
